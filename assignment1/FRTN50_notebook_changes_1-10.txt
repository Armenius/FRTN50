The problem is the final expression for \(\iota_S^*\). You currently use things like

and conditions like s >= 0. Both \(b_i\) and \(s_i\) are scalars, and the sign decision is made componentwise.

Since the maximization can be done componentwise, for each component we have

4. Task 4 — correct, but prove minimality for \(\beta^*\) too

Everything you have about

\[
\beta=\lambda_{\max}(Q)
\]

and

\[
\beta^*=\frac1{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}
\]

is correct.

You prove that \(\beta\) is the smallest possible constant, but the question asks this for both \(f\) and \(f^*\). Add the analogous argument for \(\beta^*\):

The same argument shows that \(\beta^*\) is also the smallest possible
smoothness constant. Let \(v_{\min}\neq0\) be an eigenvector of \(Q\)
corresponding to \(\lambda_{\min}(Q)\). Then

\[
Q^{-1}v_{\min}
=
\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}(Q)}v_{\min}
=
\beta^*v_{\min}.
\]

Choose \(s,t\) such that \(s-t=v_{\min}\). Then

\[
\|\nabla f^*(s)-\nabla f^*(t)\|_2
=
\|Q^{-1}v_{\min}\|_2
=
\beta^*\|v_{\min}\|_2.
\]

Thus any constant smaller than \(\beta^*\) violates the smoothness inequality,
so \(\beta^*\) is the smallest possible smoothness constant.

After that Task 4 is complete.
